The top 5 SendGrid alternatives in 2026

SendGrid alternative picks come down to how cleanly you can swap the email layer without rewriting application code. We compared five options on API compatibility, webhook fidelity and real cost at volume so engineering leads can decide fast. The v3 Mail Send endpoint allows 10,000 requests per second and up to 1,000 recipients per call. DMARC alignment and dedicated IP reputation affect deliverability at scale.

The short answer

Choose Mailgun if you want a drop-in REST API with a permanent free tier and built-in deliverability tooling. Choose Postmark if transactional reliability, template-first workflows and 45-day log retention matter most. Choose Amazon SES when you need pay-as-you-go pricing at scale and can operate the sandbox-to-production path. Choose SuprSend if you want to keep your existing provider accounts but add workflows, batching, multi-channel routing and a preference centre on top. Each option handles Web Push and mobile push via APNs or FCM differently, so match the channel mix to your product.

Mailgun

Mailgun is a developer-first email API and SMTP service with a forever free tier of 100 emails per day and paid plans starting at $15/month for 10,000 emails. Its REST API and SMTP relay are compatible with SendGrid-style integration patterns, and the platform includes a visual template editor, inbound routing and a deliverability suite called Mailgun Optimize. Rate limits sit at 1,500 API requests per second per client IP with no concurrent connection caps.

Pros: Forever free tier (100 emails/day); deliverability suite with inbox placement testing and blocklist monitoring. Cons: Log retention 1–30 days depending on plan; dedicated IPs from $59/month extra. Pricing: Free tier 100 emails/day; paid from $15/month for 10,000 emails, dedicated IPs from $59/month.

Postmark

Postmark focuses exclusively on transactional email with a template-first API that encourages separating layouts from code. Every account starts on a free Developer plan with 100 emails per month that never expires. Paid plans begin at $15/month for 10,000 emails with overage at $1.80 per 1,000 on Basic and $1.30 per 1,000 on Pro. Message retention is 45 days by default, extendable to 365 days on higher tiers. The API accepts up to 50 recipients per message and 5 MB per body part.

Pros: Permanent free tier (100 emails/month); template-first API with MessageID correlation for webhooks. Cons: No marketing/bulk streams on Free plan; 50-recipient limit per API call. Pricing: Free 100 emails/month; Basic $15/month for 10,000 emails, overage $1.80/1k; Pro $16.50/month, overage $1.30/1k.

Amazon SES

Amazon SES introduced three hierarchical pricing plans (Essentials, Pro and Enterprise) in July 2026, each bundling capabilities that were previously sold à-la-carte. Essentials adds deliverability insights, Pro adds managed dedicated IPs, email validation and global inbox placement visibility, while Enterprise adds multi-region resilience and workload-level reputation isolation. New accounts start in a sandbox limited to verified identities and daily volume caps; moving out requires a support request. The v2 API accepts messages up to 40 MB (throttled above 10 MB) and identities are capped at 10,000 per region.

Pros: Pay-as-you-go base rates; new plans bundle validation and dedicated IPs. Cons: Sandbox graduation required; you manage deliverability tooling yourself. Pricing: Pay-as-you-go base rates; Essentials/Pro/Enterprise plans with bundled features (see AWS SES pricing page for plan pricing).

SuprSend

SuprSend is not a sending vendor. It sits above your existing SendGrid, Mailgun, SES or other provider accounts and adds workflows, batching, digests, smart routing, a preference centre and multi-tenant data modelling. You keep paying your providers directly while SuprSend handles orchestration, tenant overrides and cross-channel fallbacks. The Free plan includes 10,000 notifications/month across all channels; Essentials at $100/month billed annually raises that to 50,000 and removes inbox branding; Business at $250/month billed annually unlocks batching, digests, preferences and objects.

Pros: Keeps your current provider contracts; adds workflow depth, multi-tenancy and preferences without code changes. Cons: Batching, digests and preferences require Business plan ($250/mo); multi-tenancy is Enterprise or paid add-on; smaller brand than SendGrid. Pricing: Free 10k notifications/mo; Essentials $100/mo billed annually (50k); Business $250/mo billed annually (50k + batching/digests/preferences).

SendGrid (baseline)

SendGrid remains the reference point: a 60-day free trial at 100 emails per day, then Essentials from $19.95/month, Pro from $89.95/month and custom Premier pricing. The v3 Mail Send endpoint allows 10,000 requests per second and up to 1,000 recipients per call. Dedicated IPs are included on Pro and Premier; subuser management and SSO appear on higher tiers. If your volume and feature needs fit Essentials or Pro, staying put is often the simplest path.

Pros: Mature ecosystem, extensive client libraries, 10k req/sec v3 API limit. Cons: Free trial expires in 60 days; dedicated IPs only on Pro/Premier; marketing features require separate Marketing Campaigns product. Pricing: Free trial 100 emails/day for 60 days; Essentials from $19.95/mo; Pro from $89.95/mo; Premier custom.
