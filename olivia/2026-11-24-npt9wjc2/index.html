Push vs in-app notifications: when to use each

Product managers choose between push and in-app notifications by matching urgency and the user's read state to the right channel. Push interrupts immediately; an in-app inbox waits until the user opens the product. Get it right, and critical alerts reach users in seconds while routine updates stay organized. Get it wrong, and users disable permissions or miss time-sensitive actions.

The short answer

Choose OneSignal if you need free mobile push up to 1,000 MAU and simple cross-channel journeys. Choose SuprSend when you want a unified workflow layer with an in-app inbox, preference centre and multi-tenant support on one platform. Choose Braze for enterprise-grade orchestration and predictive AI at scale. Choose CleverTap if real-time behavioural segmentation and CleverAI tokens matter most. Choose MoEngage when transactional alerts across SMS, email and push need a dedicated infrastructure product.

OneSignal

OneSignal is a cross-channel messaging platform that leads with mobile push and adds email, SMS and in-app messaging through its Journeys builder. The free tier covers unlimited mobile push and in-app messages for up to 1,000 monthly active users, making it a common starting point for early-stage apps. Paid plans unlock more Journeys, segments and longer reporting history.

Pros: Generous free tier for push; visual journey builder with branching.

Cons: In-app inbox is basic compared to dedicated feed products; advanced personalization gated to annual plans.

Pricing: Free tier; Growth starts at $19/mo. For more info, visit their pricing page.

SuprSend

SuprSend is a notification infrastructure layer that sits above your email, SMS, WhatsApp and push providers. It gives you a visual workflow builder with batching, digests and smart channel routing, plus a drop-in in-app inbox with tabs, read/unread state and a hosted preference centre. Multi-tenancy with sub-tenants and per-tenant vendors is available on Enterprise or as a paid add-on.

Pros: Deep workflow logic (branching, batching, digests); in-app inbox and preferences built in; self-hosted and BYOC options.

Cons: Batching, digests and preferences require Business plan ($250/mo billed annually); multi-tenancy gated to Enterprise; smaller brand than Braze or OneSignal.

Pricing: Essentials $100/mo billed annually (50k notifications). For more info, visit their pricing page.

Braze

Braze is a customer engagement platform built for large consumer brands. Its Canvas journey builder supports cross-channel campaigns with predictive AI, intelligent timing and personalization paths, though AI features are gated to Pro and Enterprise editions. Pricing scales with monthly active users, starting at $75/mo for 5,000 MAU on the Essentials plan.

Pros: Sophisticated journey orchestration; strong AI personalization at higher tiers; extensive integration ecosystem.

Cons: No public pricing above entry tier; AI features excluded from Essentials; steep learning curve for smaller teams.

Pricing: Essentials from $75/mo for 5,000 MAU. For more info, visit their pricing page.

CleverTap

CleverTap combines a real-time customer data platform with omnichannel orchestration. The Core plan starts at ₹2,000/mo and includes CleverAI tokens for assisted orchestration, behavioural segmentation and experimentation. Channels such as email, SMS and WhatsApp are sold as add-ons, so costs grow with channel count.

Pros: Real-time segmentation with up to 10 years of data retention; CleverAI tokens included from day one; TesseractDB for live signals.

Cons: Add-on model makes total cost harder to predict; API concurrency limits per account; smaller free tier than OneSignal.

Pricing: Core ₹2,000/mo. For more info, visit their pricing page.

MoEngage

MoEngage splits its offering into Inform for real-time transactional alerts and Personalize for marketing campaigns. Inform delivers time-sensitive alerts across SMS, email and push with fallback switching and unified reporting. Both products use custom pricing; there is no public free tier or self-serve plan.

Cons: No public pricing or free tier; rate limits and payload caps on REST APIs; requires sales engagement to evaluate.

Pricing: Custom pricing. For more info, visit their pricing page.

Push works when the user is away and the message cannot wait. In-app works when the user is already in the product and the message can wait for the next session. The quadrant chart above turns that rule into a quick decision tool. Match your urgency and read-state reality to the tool that handles the channel best.
